# C1: periodic geometry in the DLM output distribution

C1 uses logits and hidden states from the same DLM forward pass to predict a joint distribution over the current coordinate axis. A learned Fourier interaction scores periodic differences along a fixed tree:

$$q(z\mid s)\propto \exp\left[\frac{\sum_i\ell_i(z_i;s)+\sum_{(i,j)\in T}g_\psi(z_i-z_j;h(s),L)}{\tau}\right].$$

Tree message passing computes the partition function, conditional marginals and joint samples in `O(NQ²)` with `Q=100`. The existing reveal order commits the selected token, updates context and reruns the DLM. Learned compatibility therefore influences the candidate distribution inside the actual construction process.

The formula is conditioned on the visible coordinates and the caller's supported values. Coordinate token aliases 000 and 100 are merged with log-sum-exp **before** temperature; excluded support has zero probability. The same temperature scales unary and edge potentials. [Complete Chinese derivation and teacher story](../C1_C2_METHOD_ZH.md#c1).

## Periodic parameterization and inference

For zero-based site indices, the default tree has parent $p(j)=\lfloor(j-1)/2\rfloor$ for $j=1,\ldots,N-1$. A small network predicts edge coefficients from both sites' normalized DLM hidden states and element embeddings, the normalized lattice Gram matrix, log volume, current axis, and jointly visible transverse-coordinate differences with visibility masks:

$$
g_{\psi,pj}(d;s)=B\tanh\left[
\frac1{\sqrt H}\sum_{m=1}^{H}
\left(\alpha_{pjm}(s)\cos\frac{2\pi md}{Q}
+\beta_{pjm}(s)\sin\frac{2\pi md}{Q}\right)\right].
$$

Defaults are $H=8$ and $B=4$. Active-axis values are supplied as evidence, not direct numerical edge features; the DLM hidden states still contain the legitimate visible context. These learned log potentials are compatibility scores, not physical energies.

For fixed potentials, an upward tree message is

$$
m_{j\to p}(z_p)=
\log\sum_{z_j}
\exp\left[
\frac{\ell_j(z_j)+g_{\psi,pj}((z_p-z_j)\bmod Q)}{\tau}
+\sum_{k\in\operatorname{ch}(j)}m_{k\to j}(z_j)
\right].
$$

Disallowed values, including conflicting evidence, are excluded. The root gives the partition function; downward messages and ancestral sampling give conditional marginals and joint candidates. Exactness concerns this tree with this forward pass's fixed potentials and support, subject to numerical precision. The fixed slot tree is not permutation equivariant; periodic edge differences alone do not imply invariance of the entire context-dependent DLM distribution.

## Candidates, commits and the actual transition

Construction fixes composition, generates lattice fields, then follows the X/Y/Z schedule grouped by element. Current feedback assets retain `legacy_unary` confidence: at each jointly sampled candidate token, the constructor reads its untempered unary softmax score and uses the original active-group commit rule. The optional `joint_marginal` confidence policy is not credited to this experiment.

Only the chosen positions are committed. Uncommitted joint values are auxiliary draws, so the recorded candidate score $\log q(z\mid s)$ is not the probability of the projected transition. If $\Phi_s(z)$ maps a joint candidate to the state after the commit, the transition would require

$$
K_{\theta,\psi}(s'\mid s)=
\sum_zq_{\theta,\psi}(z\mid s)\mathbf1\{\Phi_s(z)=s'\}.
$$

The implementation does not evaluate this marginalization as an exact trajectory likelihood. After every commit it reruns the DLM and rebuilds the law. C1 introduces no additional DLM call just to compute its head; the inherited geometry support and recovery remain part of the constructor.

## C1's own training objective

When training the periodic head itself, DLM parameters $\theta_0$ are frozen. Original training crystals provide views with earlier axes and a current-axis prefix visible, and remaining coordinates masked:

$$
\mathcal L_{\rm C1}(\psi)=
\mathbb E_{(c,y),a,\mathrm{cut}}
\left[-\log q_{\theta_0,\psi}^{(a)}
(z^{\rm target}\mid s^{\rm target}_{a,\mathrm{cut}})\right].
$$

This conditional joint NLL trains the head. It is distinct from the subsequent C2 feedback objective, which trains DLM LoRA with typed-unary reconstruction while keeping the selected head fixed.

## Cooperation with C2 feedback

In the current [C2 feedback route](c2.md), C1 supplies the actual construction states: the original prompt, sampled lattice, visible coordinates and recorded coordinate commits. C2 uses these states to build physically checked supervision. Complete-teacher fitting also trains lattice prediction, so feedback is not restricted to coordinates.

The evaluated feedback recipe updates DLM LoRA parameters while keeping C1 parameters fixed. C1 nevertheless receives different logits and hidden states from the updated DLM and rebuilds its conditional periodic distribution at each commit. This is how learned feedback influences subsequent positions without an online physical tool or a separate learned verifier in this recipe. The joint distribution is per coordinate axis; it is not a claim of exact joint inference over lattice and all three axes simultaneously. Periodic compatibility and structural validity do not by themselves certify thermodynamic or mechanical stability.

Training fits joint coordinate likelihood on partial views of training crystals. The DLM stays frozen while the periodic head learns. Defaults are one epoch, batch 16, microbatch 4, Adam LR `1e-4`, width 64, eight harmonics and potential bound 4. Validation likelihood selects the head.

bash scripts/sample.sh c1 --config configs/local.json --plans H1A2_1050
